AI in Education

Does AI help you learn, or just help you finish?

The best current evidence says the same thing two ways: AI that does your thinking for you lowers what you learn, and AI used as a tutor mostly doesn’t. The post below is from our technology director; the resources after it add the original research behind the headlines, so you can read the source instead of the summary.

From Ms. Gjedsted

Let’s talk about technology in education! Here is the current discourse on AI in education that has come up in my algorithms:

  • The term cognitive surrender is mentioned in this MIT Report on AI and Education and sensationalized in other articles (see NYTimes: An MIT Report Warns AI Is Causing Cognitive Surrender. Universities Are In A Bind). However, I find the original report optimistic and a call to return to good teaching and learning in higher education. What are your thoughts on the original report?
  • The Economist published an article (Does AI stop children from learning?) that uses a study frequently referenced (also noted in the NYTimes article). Most references talk about the first finding: homework scores rose and homework time fell for students who used AI, but exam scores fell for a large percentage of them. There is another part to the study. Students who used AI but spent as long on their homework didn’t experience the same drop in exam scores. The article concludes:
    “What matters, then, is how pupils use the technology. Those whose exam results remained strong were not simply copying and pasting answers to save time. More likely they used the chatbots as a personal tutor, perhaps to explain difficult concepts or help solve specific problems.”

  • What do you want teachers to know about your AI use?
  • The penalty study used time spent on homework as its signal. When you build your game with AI, what would tell you whether you’re learning or outsourcing?

How AI Actually Works

What’s really happening when a chatbot answers or an image generator draws?

A chatbot is a very large math function that predicts the next chunk of text, trained on more writing than any person could read in thousands of years. Image and video tools work differently: they start from random noise and remove it step by step until a picture appears. Knowing this helps you predict when your AI coding partner will be confidently wrong, and why your generated sprites come out with six-fingered hands.

Start with the short video, then go deeper if you’re curious.

  • Image generators learned from millions of existing pictures. What does that suggest about how “original” their output can be?
  • Even the people who build these models can’t fully explain how they reach an answer. Does that change how much you trust them?

AI in Coding & Software

What do you actually need to know about code and computer science to build software with AI agents?

By early 2026, some professional developers said AI agents were writing around 80% of their code. That doesn’t make code knowledge useless; it changes which parts matter. AI is good at the first draft. The rest is still on you: deciding exactly what to build, describing it precisely, reading and testing what the AI wrote, catching security holes, and understanding how the pieces of your game fit together. That’s where computer science pays off.

Resources

  • Start here

    The 70% problem: Hard truths about AI-assisted coding

    Addy Osmani, December 2024

    Essay10–30 min

    A Google engineering leader explains why AI gets you about 70% of the way fast, while the last 30% (edge cases, security, connecting the parts) is as hard as ever. Explains why beginners get stuck in a loop of fixes that break other things.

  • The 80% Problem in Agentic Coding

    Addy Osmani, January 2026

    Essay10–30 min

    The sequel, written once AI agents could do more. The share AI writes went up, but the hard part moved rather than disappeared: AI’s mistakes are now wrong assumptions rather than typos, and the human’s job is defining the problem and checking the result.

  • Software Is Changing (Again)

    Andrej Karpathy, Y Combinator, June 2025

    Talk30+ min

    A founding member of OpenAI, speaking to students, describes three kinds of software: code people write, neural networks trained on data, and AI you program in plain English. His advice is to be fluent in all three and know which one fits a problem.

  • Here’s how I use LLMs to help me write code

    Simon Willison, March 2025

    Practical guide30+ min

    A respected programmer’s honest how-to: set realistic expectations, give the AI context, and always test what it writes. Shows what working knowledge he relies on when the AI is wrong.

  • How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills

    Anthropic, January 2026

    Research summary10–30 min

    Names the specific skills you need to supervise AI-written code (debugging, reading code and understanding concepts) and found they suffered most when people handed off the work. People who asked for explanations kept them. Written by researchers at a company that sells AI coding tools.

  • Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Developer Productivity

    METR, July 2025

    Research summary10–30 min

    Experienced programmers were 19% slower with AI on their own projects but believed they were 20% faster. The February 2026 follow-up suggests newer tools help more, but so many developers refused to work without AI that it couldn’t be measured cleanly.

  • No Widespread Displacement, but the AI Employment Gap for Young Workers Has Widened

    Stanford Digital Economy Lab, August 2026

    Research summaryUnder 10 min

    Jobs for 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed fields, including software development, are 19% below where they’d otherwise be. The drop is in work AI replaces; where AI helps people do their job, employment held steady. Which side of that line you’re on depends on your skills.

  • 2026 State of the Game Industry

    GDC, 2026

    Industry surveyUnder 10 min

    52% of 2,300 game professionals think generative AI is hurting the industry, and game programmers (59%) are among the most negative. Yet about a third use it at work, mostly for research and code help rather than finished game content.

  • A Coder Considers the Waning Days of the Craft

    James Somers, The New Yorker, November 2023

    Personal essay (paywalled)30+ min

    A programmer asks what’s left of his craft when AI can write code. Written before AI agents existed; compare his predictions with the 2026 pieces above.

Questions to consider

  • AI can write the first 70–80% of your game. What’s in the last 20–30%: the bug that only appears on level 3, the score that doesn’t save, the collision that sometimes fails? What would you need to know to fix it?
  • Karpathy says English is now a programming language. What makes a description of a game feature precise enough for an AI to build it the way you imagined?
  • Which computer science ideas (game state, loops, events, data structures, how a game updates each frame) did you need to understand when the AI got something wrong?
  • Entry-level jobs are shrinking where AI replaces the work and holding steady where it helps people do the work. What skills would put you on the second side?

AI in Art & Design

What does using AI for game art mean for artists, players and ownership?

Game artists are the group most opposed to generative AI: 64% of visual and technical artists in the 2026 GDC survey said it is hurting the industry. Players are watching too. Since January 2026, Steam requires games to label AI-generated art, audio and writing that players see or hear, while code written with an AI assistant needs no label. So every art choice in your game is also a choice about how you present it.

If you haven’t yet, watch the Welch Labs video on how image generators work in How AI Actually Works first.

    Game Developer, January 2026

    The rules a real game store applies to AI content, including the line between AI tools used to build a game and AI content shipped to players. Useful if you ever publish your game.

  • Copyright and Artificial Intelligence

    U.S. Copyright Office

    Government reports30+ min

Questions to consider

  • If you used AI for any art, sound or writing in your game, how would you describe it in a Steam-style disclosure? Would players react differently than you expect?
  • If a prompt-only image can’t be copyrighted, who owns the AI-generated sprite in your game? What would you need to add to make it yours?
  • Where in your game does hand-made work matter most to you, and where would you be happy to use a generated placeholder?
  • What’s the difference between AI “slop” and a creative tool? Is it the tool, the effort, or the intent?

Ethical Questions Around AI

Who gets paid, what does it cost the planet, and who decides the rules?

The biggest ethical questions about AI are still open in 2026: courts haven’t decided whether training on copyrighted work is legal, experts disagree about the environmental cost, and governments are only starting to set rules. That makes this a good topic to argue about. Several resources below are deliberately paired so you hear both sides, and each of the four topics has its own starting point.

    Where the Times’ 2023 lawsuit stands now: both sides have asked the judge to rule on “fair use” without a trial. Short and clear on what each side argues.

    NPR, September 2025

    News articleUnder 10 min

    The $1.5 billion settlement over pirated books used for training. A judge had ruled that training on purchased books was fair use but downloading pirated copies was not.

    Authors Guild, July 2026

    UpdateUnder 10 min

    The follow-up: the settlement was approved in July 2026, at roughly $3,000 per book. Written by an authors’ advocacy group, so note its point of view.

Questions to consider

  • The AI you used to build your game learned from other people’s code, art and writing. Do you owe those creators anything? What would fair look like?
  • After reading both Hao and Masley, what evidence would change your mind about AI’s environmental cost?
  • An AI company CEO calling for regulation: is that responsibility, strategy, or both?
  • If you added an AI-powered character to your game that talks to players, what guardrails would you need, especially for younger players?
